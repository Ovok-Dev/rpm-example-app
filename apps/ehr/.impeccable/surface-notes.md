# EHR first-surface composition round

Product mode: Operate. Confirmed lead task: clinic operations overview. The established Ovok world comes from `../dashboard/DESIGN.md`: cool lavender workspace, one white clinical reading surface per coherent task, strong ink, purple navigation/action, clear source and timestamp, quiet lines, and the existing sans-serif hierarchy. Keep that world fixed; this round only decides information structure.

## Seven grounded structures, ordered by resonance

1. Patient review register: a concise set of patient rows, each carrying next task, latest reading, assigned clinician, and a direct chart action. Fast to scan and familiar in a clinical setting.
2. Longitudinal chart rail: a selected patient's dated events run down one clear timeline, with overview details and history aligned beside it. Best for understanding one patient's record, not clinic-wide operations.
3. Intake packet: a patient's registration, history, contacts, and consent travel as a sequence of clearly named sections with incomplete fields visible. Best for finishing intake, not the first operations view.
4. Care-team assignment roster: practitioner availability and assigned-patient counts sit beside a compact reassignment queue. Makes responsibility visible but over-centers staffing.
5. Enrollment workbench: patient, CHF program, responsible clinician, questionnaire schedule, and kit assignment appear in one staged workflow. Best for activation, not the daily operations overview.
6. Care-coordination task ledger: dated care tasks, due dates, owners, and patient references form a work queue with completion preserved in history. Strong for follow-up and operational accountability.
7. Daily clinic rounds sheet: today's work and appointments lead, with awaiting-intake, unassigned-patient, active-enrollment, and kit availability figures kept in a narrow, labeled summary. The work queue opens each patient's chart and keeps urgent operational gaps visible without implying clinical risk.

## The dealt hand

The seed selected grounded structures 7, 5, and 1. The first card is the roll. These all retain Operations Overview as the initial purpose while varying the route through the information.

## Challenger judgments

- Split-flap board: competitive. The fixed columns and stateful rows fit an operational patient queue and preserve scanability, but its transit-board metaphor is less recognizable to a care team than the grounded clinic sheet. Keep grid discipline and clear state changes; reject its dark/amber identity.
- Reference-setting page: declined. The central keyed rail is visually disciplined but the two-column reading form slows daily operational scanning. Keep source and provenance adjacent to the record they qualify.
- Design annual plate: declined. Its provenance grammar is compatible with record authorship, but the plate layout does not clarify the clinic's next action. Keep source, author, and timestamp visible.
- Streaming catalog wall: declined. Focus expansion does not suit dense clinic operations. Keep keyboard-first patient search and focus.
- Labanotation: declined. Time-coded alignment has limited use for event history but does not identify as clinical operations. Keep chronological ordering and event timestamps.
- Airline ticket wallet: declined. Its stepwise route supports enrollment but frames clinical work as travel. Keep the explicit, recoverable enrollment sequence.

No clinical state, alert severity, device logistics, or backend capability is invented by this composition round. Synthetic patients and operational counts must be identified as examples.
